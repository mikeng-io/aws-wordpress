# E0 at n=10 — the revalidation tax, and the floor beneath it

**Result:** `results/E0/20260827T185901Z-bff2d22/`
**n:** 10 per cell (3 for `warm-aged`)

## Corrects the first run

The [first run](E0-first-run.md) concluded that opcache tuning "barely dents" the
stat storm. That conclusion was an artefact of the apparatus: both profiles were
measured *inside* their revalidation window, where neither revalidates, so of course
they matched.

With a `warm-aged` cohort that waits past the window, they separate sharply.

## The model

Syscall counts turn out to be **deterministic** — at n=10 nearly every cell has a
zero-width range. Three components, on a home request:

| Component | stat calls | When paid |
|---|--:|---|
| Compile (cold) | ~10,600 | first request after php-fpm master start |
| Revalidation tax | ~+4,400 | once per `revalidate_freq` window, if validation on |
| **Plugin floor** | **~3,900** | **every request, always** |

- `max` (`validate_timestamps=0`): ~3,900 stats on every warm request, forever.
- `tuned` / `naive` (validation on): ~3,900 inside the window, **~8,300** on the
  request that crosses it.

Both validating profiles pay the *same* tax when they revalidate — `naive` aged is
~7,666 stats, `tuned` aged ~8,331. They differ only in **how often**:
`naive` every 2s, `tuned` every 60s. So the amortised cost falls as traffic rises,
and opcache tuning matters most to *low-traffic* sites, which is the opposite of the
usual intuition.

## The floor is the finding

Even with timestamp validation off entirely, a warm request still issues **~3,900
stat calls and ~4,300 filesystem syscalls total**. That is plugin code running its
own `file_exists` / `is_readable` / template-hierarchy checks — wpforms-lite alone
~1,155 ops, against ~103 for all of `wp-includes`.

**`php.ini` cannot go below it.** And the governing constraint forbids touching the
plugins.

Roughly **1,600 of those ops are ENOENT** — a third to two-fifths of warm ops are
lookups for files that do not exist, but the share is not flat: `naive` sits at
~30–33% across endpoints, `max`/`tuned` at ~33–38%, a consistent 4–6 percentage-point
gap in every endpoint (home, product, wp-admin, cart). Turning validation off doesn't
just fail to shrink the floor — it modestly *raises* the ENOENT share within it,
plausibly because the requests validation would have short-circuited on a timestamp
match now run further into the same failed-lookup chains.

## Bearing on the study

The multiplier that per-op latency gets applied to is ~4,300 warm and ~15,400 cold,
and it is irreducible from inside the application. That is what makes
[H1](../../hypotheses/H1-cache-locality.md) worth testing: if the op count is fixed,
only where those ops are *served from* remains as a lever.

It does not resolve [H3](../../hypotheses/H3-opcache-dominates.md). H3's kill
condition is about the spread across storage backends at max tuning, and E0 measures
no storage backends. E0 constrains H3; only E2 can settle it.

## Limitations

1. Generated catalog — `wp post generate` products have no media, so `uploads/`
   and image-size paths are under-exercised.
2. `warm-aged` has n=3, not 10 (each sample costs a 63s wait).
3. Counts, not latency. Nothing here says anything about EFS.
